The Anthropocene

The Anthropocene, a geological era named after humans for the significant impact they have on the environment. While many would point toward a spike in carbon content in the atmosphere and the burning of fossil fuels as the driver of the Anthropocene, I argue that the Great Acceleration actually starts right when nuclear technology is being developed, with the spike beginning in the 1950s as constant nuclear experiments were conducted.

Hibakusha

The victims of nuclear weapons and radioactive disasters, the trauma of such technology has been scorched into their psyches. It is not a solely Japanese concept, but a study of the Hibakusha reveals the social anxieties around nuclear technology and demonstrates a very clear lesson of what occurs if we're not careful with it. It is also a way of dealing with memory and trauma, and should not be disregarded as we potentially move forward with nuclear development. After all, those who don't learn from history are doomed to repeat it.

Nuclear disaster in popular imagination

A study of films, games and literature depicting nuclear apocalypses and why it's important. They depict the social anxieties the public face when confronted with nuclear technologies and disasters, and should not be dismissed as merely fiction. Probably Tom Moylan's dystopia books and Timothy Morton's Hyperobjects in academia, and analyses of Japanese films and animation such as Godzilla, Barefoot Gen, Gundam, Akira and many others touch upon it.
