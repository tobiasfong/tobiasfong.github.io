Background

The one step I was still outsourcing

Every other piece of this ecosystem runs on my own GPU at zero cost — backgrounds, characters, parallax, video. Music was the exception. The anime production skill could always play an mp3, but never make one, and that mp3 came from Suno: a paid cloud service, the same category as Kling for video.

It was also the only part of a trailer I couldn't iterate on freely, because every attempt cost money. That's a worse constraint than it sounds. You don't stop at the take you like; you stop at the take you can justify paying for again.

This closes that gap. Theme songs and BGM now generate locally on the same 6 GB laptop GPU as everything else.

How It Works

ACE-Step, through ComfyUI

ACE-Step is the only strong open model that does songs with vocals — MusicGen and Stable Audio Open are instrumental-only, which is fine for BGM and useless for a theme song. It runs through ComfyUI, which has supported it natively since v0.25, so there's nothing to install but the model weights. That made it a materially easier setup than the local video path, which needed third-party loaders.

The server takes style tags, lyrics, tempo, key, time signature and language, and returns a lossless FLAC to archive plus a 320k MP3 the Remotion pipeline can drop straight in. It also emits a beat grid, so trailer cuts land on downbeats rather than wherever the edit happened to fall.

It runs faster than real time — a 107-second track renders in about 90 seconds at roughly 5.9 GB of VRAM. That changes how you work with it. Instead of agonising over one attempt, you generate five and pick the performance you like.

What I Learned

The interesting part wasn't the generation — it was working out what the controls do

  • Lyric density is arithmetic, and it's the master control. One bar is 4 × 60 / bpm seconds. Divide the track's bars by its sung lines: land near 2.3 bars per line and lines get sung as lines. At 4.7 they stretch into fragments with gaps. Below 2 they run together, and trailing lines get silently dropped.
  • Instrumental sections aren't free duration. They compete with the lyric for the same bars. Adding an intro, three interludes and an outro to an 85-second track quietly truncated the song at line 10 of 16.
  • Asking for a relative minor does nothing. I picked B minor to keep the mood "related" to the D major I had. B minor is D major's seven notes — that's what a relative pair is. Sharing a pitch collection is exactly what made it unable to sound less bright.
  • Some things aren't controllable at all. Vocal register ignores the prompt: five takes at identical settings returned one female vocal and one noticeably high-pitched, despite explicit deep male baritone tags. Sample and pick; don't tune.
Why use this?

Because iteration being free changes what you'll settle for

Local, free, and unlimited. No subscription, no per-generation cost, and no terms-of-service question about who owns the output. Because iteration costs nothing, you can chase a specific feeling across a dozen takes rather than accepting the first usable one.

The honest limit: no statistic tells you whether a vocal is any good. The tooling measures tempo, key and structure; the ear does the rest. That's a human-in-the-loop workflow by design, same as the rest of the ecosystem — the tools narrow the search, the author still chooses.

Release Notes

What's new

v0.2.0 Beat grids without Node, and a settled theme song

August 6, 2026

  • 🥁 Changed — beat extraction is pure Python now. It was a Node script that shelled out to ffmpeg; it's now NumPy and soundfile, which bundles libsndfile, so neither dependency is needed at all. Same output schema, so it's still a Remotion drop-in. On the winning track: 268 beats, 67 downbeats, a 1.600 s bar — and 67 bars is exactly what the density arithmetic predicted for 107 seconds, which is a nice independent check on the maths above.
  • 📐 Changed — tempo comes from the recipe, not from detection. When a track was generated here, the BPM was an input. Detecting it again can only be worse.
  • 🎼 Added — reference analysis. Measures the BPM and key of any mp3/flac/wav so those parameters come from measurement rather than guesswork. It can't separate relative major/minor pairs — nothing chroma-based can — and says so when it sees one. That limitation is what proved requesting "B minor" returns D major's pitch collection.
  • ✅ Added — approve_track / forget_track. Track ids carry a timestamp so auditioning can't clobber a good take. Approving publishes the winner under a FINAL_ name, which the toolkit's "never delete an approved FINAL_" rule then covers automatically — and forget_track refuses an approved take in code. Worth having: 22 tracks were script-deleted in that same session.
  • 🔒 Fixed — a near-miss, one command before the first push. The .gitignore had an exception to preserve the track manifest — but recipes embed lyrics, and the repo is public, so it would have published my unreleased song text. The output folder is ignored wholesale now, with the reason recorded in the file.

Earlier releases

v0.1.0 First cut — and 6 GB turned out to be enough August 6, 2026
  • 🎤 Six tools — generate a track, generate variations, list and fetch tracks, extract a beat grid, check status. Plus a standalone CLI driver for sweeping settings without MCP in the way.
  • 🧠 ACE-Step 1.5 over 1.0. 1.5 is the bigger download (10.0 GB against 7.7), because it needs two text encoders rather than one. Chosen anyway: it's the only variant with an explicit language input, and Japanese vocals were the whole point. 1.0 has no way to declare a language and infers it from the lyric script.
  • 💾 6 GB is enough — verified, not assumed. The plan said "assume quantisation will be needed." No quantisation exists or is needed; ComfyUI streams the bf16 weights. Peak ~5.9 GB for a two-minute track, no OOM. --lowvram made no measurable difference either way, so it's off.
  • ⏱️ Faster than real time, which inverted the assumption that auditioning would be expensive. Five variations take about nine minutes.
  • 🎧 Both formats from one sampling pass — lossless FLAC to keep, MP3 for the video pipeline. The extra encode is free next to the sampling.
  • 🧪 28 offline checks, no GPU required.
Ecosystem

The last outsourced step in the pipeline

This is the newest piece of a wider webcomic and animation toolkit: the Background Generator for scenery, the Character & Panel Generator for consistent figures, Novel Translation for the Japanese edition, and Anime Production for the video itself — with a speech bubble server still to come. With music in place, a trailer can now be made end to end without a single paid service: art, song, animation, all on one machine.

For everyone's use

Get the server

It's open source (MIT), like the rest of the toolkit. Ask your AI harness to clone the webcomic-toolkit repository and follow the setup docs in music-generation-mcp. You'll need ComfyUI and the ACE-Step weights; there are no custom nodes to install, and a 6 GB card is enough.
